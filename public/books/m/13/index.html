三元法学

Triadic Law
A Genesis Model for the Unification of All Laws
以SIO三元结构重写法学基础：法律不是规则集合，而是社会S-D-E三维的稳定机制。
内 容 简 介

本书对法学提出一次激进的重新奠基：法律首先不是规则的集合，也不是价值的冷藏箱，而是「意义之生成运动」的活的守护者。它不追问「如何在事后为规范辩护」，而追问一个更根本的问题——「法」本身如何诞生、如何稳定、又为何必须反复更新才能保持其为法。核心主张是：一切法律秩序、道德秩序、语言秩序与逻辑秩序，都源于同一个发生——意义的三元动力学。

展 开 · 这本书在讲什么

这套动力学经由三条不可分割的原理展开。其一，幸福原理：任何真实互动都会累积张力，因而需要合法的转化与释放通道，否则系统会反弹为更高的成本与更深的冲突。其二，同一性原理：只有当差异进入一个稳定化的窗口，稳定的标准与可重复的判断才会涌现，让定义、程序与责任变得公开可辨、可复核。其三，自由原理：一套法律系统必须保住「可能的转换空间」——例外、可逆性、试验与适应性修订——好让秩序不至僵化为瘫痪。

从这个三元内核出发，本书搭起一个统一框架，能解释「法」为何以如此众多的形态出现——成文法与法庭、习俗与伦理、语法与逻辑——而不把它们贬为单纯的比喻。它进一步引入一套形式化词汇（连接、次序、结构），描述意义如何变得可被制度操作：路径如何变得可达、次序如何被协调、层级系统如何维持长期融贯。写给站在法哲学、文明理论与 AI 时代治理交叉口的读者，本书给出的不是对现有学说的改良，而是一个发生模型：一种理解「一切法律归根到底都在寻求同一件事——让意义得以延续的合法条件」的方式。

内 容 简 介 · BOOK DESCRIPTION

Triadic Law proposes a radical re-grounding of jurisprudence: law is not first a collection of rules, nor a frozen container of values, but a living safeguard for the generative movement of meaning. Rather than asking how to justify norms after the fact, this book asks a more fundamental question-how "law" itself comes into being, how it stabilizes, and why it must repeatedly renew in order to remain lawful. The central claim is that all legal orders, moral orders, linguistic orders, and logical orders arise from one shared genesis: a triadic dynamics of meaning. This dynamics unfolds through three inseparable principles.

The first is the principle of well-being: every real interaction accumulates tension and therefore requires legitimate pathways of transformation and release, otherwise the system rebounds into higher costs and deeper conflict. The second is the principle of identity: stable criteria and repeatable judgments emerge only when differences enter a window of stabilization, allowing definitions, procedures, and responsibility to become publicly recognizable and reviewable. The third is the principle of freedom: a legal system must preserve the space of possible transitions-exceptions, reversibility, experimentation, and adaptive revision-so that order does not harden into paralysis. From this triadic core, Triadic Law builds a unified framework capable of explaining why "law" appears in so many forms-statutes and courts, customs and ethics, grammar and logic-without reducing them to mere metaphor.

It further introduces a formal vocabulary-connection, order, and structure-to describe how meaning becomes institutionally operable: how pathways are made accessible, how sequences are coordinated, and how layered systems sustain long-term coherence. Written for readers at the intersection of legal philosophy, civilizational theory, and AI-era governance, Triadic Law offers not a reform of existing doctrines but a genesis model: a way to understand why all laws, in the end, seek one thing-legitimate conditions for meaning to continue.
