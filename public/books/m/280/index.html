路加·约翰·使徒行传

一个故事写了四遍，怎样长出一个教会 · 重读圣经 · 第 17 本

不一致没有被消除，它先生成了福音，又生成了教会。

四卷福音书写同一个人，处处对不上：家谱不同，顺序不同，十字架上最后一句话不同。两千年里有人把它们合成一卷，有人只留一卷，教会两样都没要。

本书看路加怎样重写、约翰怎样另起一套，拆开「四个角度看同一件事」这种最有力的读法，再看使徒行传里，一群能容下四种讲法的人，怎样长成一个教会。最后一编把这一问带回课堂和公司：一个课堂要不要标准答案，一个公司要不要统一口径。不问信仰真伪，信的人和不信的人都能读。

Book Description

Luke, John, Acts: How a Story Told Four Times Grew a Church is volume 17 of the twenty-volume series Rereading the Bible. The four Gospels disagree on genealogies, chronology, the last words on the cross and the morning of the resurrection. Tatian merged them into one; Marcion kept only one; the church kept all four side by side. The book follows Luke the rewriter and John who began anew, then takes on the most powerful traditional reading — four perspectives on one event, from the four living creatures to Vern Poythress — and sets it beside Nietzsche's perspectivism: both presuppose a viewer who can freely choose where to stand. Its claim: the "one event" was formed by four accounts kept side by side for two thousand years, and each perspective grew out of who the writer was bound to, what materials he had, and what he sought to hold together. Acts then shows a community that survived by not unifying — from Pentecost to the Jerusalem council. A final part brings the question to classrooms and organizations. An appendix of some 20,000 characters engages nine traditions of biblical interpretation.
