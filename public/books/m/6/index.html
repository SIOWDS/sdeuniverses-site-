内 容 简 介 · BOOK DESCRIPTION

The History of Western Philosophy: The Lifespan of Identity offers a radical re-reading of two thousand years of Western thought. Instead of presenting philosophy as a succession of competing doctrines or a gradual march toward truth, this book proposes a different organizing principle: philosophy as the long experiment of constructing, stabilizing, exhausting, and replacing forms of identity. At the center of the book lies a simple but far-reaching claim: identity is not eternal. Every identity-conceptual, personal, institutional, or civilizational-has a lifespan.

It emerges through specific generative conditions, achieves temporary stability, enters fatigue, and eventually requires renewal or replacement. Western philosophy, viewed from this angle, becomes a history of identity-machines: systematic ways of producing sameness, certainty, and repeatability in an ever-changing world. Drawing on SIO Ontology (Subject-Interaction-Object), the book reframes identity as a product of generative interaction rather than a property of subjects or objects alone. Identity is understood as the stabilization of difference within concrete interactional structures.

Building on this foundation, the author introduces the Three Laws of Meaning-Feature, Freedom, and Happiness-to explain how identities arise, how they renew themselves, and why they collapse when renewal fails. Classic philosophers are reinterpreted not as timeless authorities but as engineers of specific identity regimes. From Greek metaphysics to modern rationalism, from German idealism to the philosophies of will and representation, each system is analyzed in terms of its identity-production strategy, its hidden structural omissions, and the limits of its lifespan. The book introduces a new analytical tool-the "identity lifespan verdict"-which allows philosophical systems to be evaluated by their generative completeness, sustainability, and capacity for renewal.

Beyond philosophy, the book speaks directly to the crises of the AI and GPT era. Today, identities can be generated endlessly, but meaning, stability, and renewal have become scarce. The core challenge of our time is no longer intelligence, but sustainability of meaning. By transforming philosophy from a discipline of explanation into a science of generative governance, The Lifespan of Identity offers a blueprint for rethinking education, research, organizations, and personal development in an age of accelerating change.

This book reconstructs Western philosophy for civilizational renewal.
